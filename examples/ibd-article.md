# Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)
### A Comprehensive Guide to Causes, Symptoms and Treatment

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) is a chronic condition that causes inflammation of the digestive tract. It primarily includes two diseases: Crohn’s disease and Ulcerative Colitis. Unlike Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS), which does not cause physical damage, IBD results in structural injury visible during endoscopy or imaging.

---

## What is IBD?

IBD involves prolonged inflammation of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract and typically occurs in periods of:
- Flare-ups (active symptoms)
- Remission (reduced or no symptoms)

The body’s immune system mistakenly attacks the gut, causing chronic inflammation and tissue damage.

---

## Types of IBD

### Crohn’s Disease
- Can affect any part of the GI tract (mouth to anus)
- Inflammation is patchy (skip lesions)
- May involve all layers of the bowel wall
- Common complications: strictures, fistulas, abscesses

### Ulcerative Colitis (UC)
- Affects only the colon and rectum
- Inflammation is continuous
- Affects only the inner lining of the colon
- Often causes bloody diarrhea

### Comparison

| Feature | Crohn's Disease | Ulcerative Colitis |
|---------|-----------------|--------------------|
| Area affected | Any part of GI tract | Colon and rectum only |
| Pattern | Patchy areas | Continuous inflammation |
| Depth | All layers of bowel wall | Inner lining only |
| Bleeding | Less common | Common |
| Surgery cure | Not a cure | Can cure by removing colon |

---

## Causes of IBD

There is no single known cause. IBD is believed to result from a combination of:

### Genetic Factors
- Up to 20% of IBD patients have a close relative with the condition

### Immune System Dysfunction
- Immune system mistakenly attacks the intestinal lining
- Gut microbiome imbalance may contribute

### Environmental Factors
| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Smoking | Worsens Crohn’s; may reduce UC severity |
| Diet | Processed and high-fat foods may increase risk |
| NSAIDs | Can trigger flares |
| Geography | More common in developed countries |

---

## Symptoms of IBD

### Gastrointestinal Symptoms
- Persistent diarrhea (may contain blood or mucus)
- Abdominal pain and cramping
- Rectal bleeding
- Urgency and incomplete bowel movements (tenesmus)
- Reduced appetite and weight loss
- Nausea and vomiting

### Symptoms Outside the Gut (Extraintestinal)
- Joint pain and arthritis
- Skin lesions and ulcers
- Eye inflammation
- Fatigue and fever
- Anemia
- Liver and bile duct issues (e.g., PSC)

---

## Diagnosis

IBD diagnosis requires a combination of tests:

| Test | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Blood tests | Detect inflammation and anemia |
| Stool tests | Identify infection and inflammatory markers |
| Colonoscopy with biopsy | Confirms presence and type of IBD |
| CT/MRI enterography | Assesses bowel damage and complications |
| Capsule endoscopy | Examines small intestine (hard to access otherwise) |

---

## Treatment

Treatment aims to control inflammation, relieve symptoms, maintain remission, and prevent complications.

### Medications

| Category | Use | Examples |
|----------|-----|----------|
| Aminosalicylates (5-ASAs) | Mild to moderate UC | Mesalamine, Sulfasalazine |
| Corticosteroids | Short-term flare control | Prednisone |
| Immunomodulators | Long-term maintenance | Azathioprine, 6-MP, Methotrexate |
| Biologics/Small Molecules | Moderate to severe cases | Infliximab, Adalimumab, Vedolizumab |

### Surgery
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** Removing the colon can cure the disease
- **Crohn’s Disease:** Surgery helps with complications but does not cure the condition

---

## Diet and Lifestyle Management

Diet cannot cure IBD, but it helps reduce symptoms and prevent flare-ups.

### During Flare-Ups
- Low-FODMAP diet
- Low-fiber and low-residue foods (e.g., white rice, pasta, cooked vegetables)
- Avoid spicy, fatty foods, caffeine, alcohol
- Small, frequent meals
- Increase hydration

### During Remission
- Gradually reintroduce fiber
- Follow a balanced diet (e.g., Mediterranean style)
- Identify personal trigger foods
- Use probiotics and prebiotics if tolerated

### Lifestyle Recommendations
- Quit smoking (especially for Crohn’s)
- Manage stress (e.g., breathing exercises, light exercise)
- Maintain regular physical activity

---

## When to Seek Medical Help

### Medical Consultation Needed If:
- Symptoms persist for more than two weeks
- Blood in stool
- Unintentional weight loss

### Emergency Symptoms:
- Severe abdominal pain
- High fever
- Heavy or continuous bleeding
- Signs of dehydration
